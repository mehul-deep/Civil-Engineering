1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROGRESS OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When opening your meeting with leadership, frame our progress around three major pillars:

1) Unified Application Architecture:
   We transitioned from disjointed spreadsheets into an integrated .NET 8 Windows Forms 
   desktop application powered by an Entity Framework Core / SQLite relational database. 
   This guarantees single-source-of-truth data consistency across the entire design lifecycle.

2) Parametric Standpipe Tank & Foundation Engines:
   - Standpipe Shell & Cone: Full parametric modeling for Single-Leg / Standpipe tanks, 
     tracking initial and final diameters, heights, and plate thicknesses across all 
     Base Cone (B1-B5) and Cylinder (C1-C18) segments.
   - Anchor Bolt Design (ACI 318-19 Appendix D): Automated calculation of tension (Nua) 
     and shear (Vua) demands per bolt, breakout strengths (Ncb, Nsa), and real-time 
     evaluation of the combined tension-shear interaction ratio:
     [ (Nua / phi*Nn) + (Vua / phi*Vn) <= 1.2 ]
   - Base Plate Design (AISC LRFD Annular Strip Method): Cantilever strip moment 
     evaluation (Mstrip), bearing stress checks (fp <= phi*Pp), required thickness 
     calculation (treq), and real-time compactness/thickness status monitoring.

3) UI/UX & Visual Precision:
   We refined interactive engineering drawing diagrams (such as fixing the Base Plate 
   dimension line and label positioning so it is 100% clear and never cut off) and 
   implemented an intuitive Dual-Output Menu System.

2. EXPLAINING THE DUAL-OUTPUT EXCEL ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================
A central talking point for your meeting is WHY we established two separate Excel 
export engines. Explain to leadership that manufacturing shop floors and structural 
engineers have fundamentally different data requirements. By building two separate 
export engines, we deliver tailored precision without clutter or confusion.

--- ARCHITECTURE FLOW ---
[SQLite Database: Tank & Foundation Entities] 
       |
       +--> [Desktop Application UI]
                   |
                   +--> Click: "Fabrication Output" ---> [Output.xlsx: 107-Column Shop Schedule]
                   |                                       --> Feeds Shop Fabricators & CNC Machines
                   |
                   +--> Click: "Engineering Summary" --> [EngineeringSummary.xlsx: 5-Section Report]
                                                           --> Feeds EORs, Clients & Permitting Authorities

A. The Shop Manufacturing Schedule ("Fabrication Output")
   - Target Audience: Steel Shop Fabricators, CNC Plate Cutting Machines, and Quality Control Inspectors.
   - Core Purpose: A strict 107-column manufacturing schedule that dictates exact physical piece sizes, 
     thicknesses, and hole drilling coordinates.
   - Key Architectural Decision: We preserved ALL 107 columns untouched. Nothing was deleted, hidden, 
     or rearranged. This ensures 100% backward compatibility with existing company macros, ERP systems, 
     and automated shop equipment.
   - What is Populated Now: Tank Designation, Capacity, Parameter Reference (TANK-3), Base Cone segment 
     dimensions (B1-B5), Cylinder segment dimensions (C1-C18), Base Plate manufacturing dimensions 
     (BPDIA, BPOR, BPIR, BPDEG, BPTHK, BPQ), and Anchor Bolt drilling coordinates (ABQ, ABHQ, ABHSD).

B. The Structural Calculation Report ("Engineering Summary")
   - Target Audience: Project Engineers, EORs, Clients, and Municipal Building Permitting Authorities.
   - Core Purpose: A standalone, professional structural calculation and capacity report verifying that 
     the design meets ACI 318-19 and AISC LRFD building codes.
   - Visual & Structural Design: Styled with executive soft-blue header banners (#D9E1F2), bold grey 
     data labels (#F2F2F2), and clean table borders across 5 distinct audit sections:
     1) Project & Tank Geometry Overview: Capacity, Designation, Base Cone & Cylinder dimensions.
     2) Governing Structural Loads & Demands: Overturning Moment (Mu), Axial Load (Pu), Base Shear (Vu), 
        and Ultimate Load Factors.
     3) Anchor Bolt Design & Breakout Check (ACI 318-19): Bolt counts, circle radii, embedment depth (hef), 
        strengths (f'c, Fy, Fu), demands per bolt, and an automated Combined Interaction Ratio Check 
        (outputting "OK" or "EXCEEDS CAPACITY").
     4) Base Plate Design & Bearing Stress (AISC LRFD): Radii, supporting area ratio (A2/A1), bearing stress (fp), 
        required vs. actual thickness, and an automated Plate Compactness Status (outputting "COMPACT / OK" 
        or alerting "CHECK THICKNESS").
     5) Material & Geotechnical Properties: Steel grade name, modulus of elasticity (E), densities, and 
        tensile yield/ultimate stresses.

3. FABRICATION OUTPUT COLUMN AUDIT & STRATEGIC COMPANY QUESTIONS
================================================================================
To prepare for your technical alignment discussion, we have audited all 107 columns in Output.xlsx. 
Below is the breakdown of the columns that currently output 0 or blank, categorized by WHY they are 
unpopulated, along with the EXACT questions you should ask the company to align on next steps.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CATEGORY 1: Shop Accessories & Erection Inputs (User Input vs. Shop Default)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
*These represent physical installation hardware on a Standpipe tank.*

Columns:
- SC, SCWT: Side Chairs & Side Chairs with Tops (Physical support brackets welded to base plate).
- BPSQ: Base Plate Shim Quantity (Leveling shims used during field erection on concrete ring wall).
- DDEG: Door Position Degree (Radial orientation angle of access door; currently defaulted to 0 deg).

Why they are 0: 
Our solver calculates structural base plates and bolts, but side chairs and shims are field installation accessories.

*** STRATEGIC QUESTIONS TO ASK THE COMPANY ***
1) "How should Side Chairs (SC, SCWT) be determined? Is there a standard shop rule-of-thumb based on 
   bolt diameter (db) and chair height, or should we add an explicit input field on the Base Plate UI 
   for the engineer to specify chair quantities and styles?"
2) "For Leveling Shims (BPSQ), does the shop assume a standard 1:1 ratio with Anchor Bolts (BPSQ = ABQ), 
   or is shim quantity strictly a field-erection value entered manually after foundation survey?"
3) "Should we add a radial orientation selector for Door Position Degree (DDEG) on the Tank Properties screen 
   (e.g., North = 0 deg, East = 90 deg), or does the shop prefer leaving it at 0 deg for standard standpipes?"

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CATEGORY 2: Ring Arc-Plate Quantities & Degrees (Shop Roll Cutting Rules)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
*These define how many individual curved steel plates are welded together to form a single circumferential ring.*

Columns:
- B1 SEGMENT DEGREE to B5...: Base Cone Arc-Plate Layout.
- B1 SEGEMENT QUANTITY to B5...: Number of Plates per Base Ring.
- Cylinder plate layouts: Arc degrees and quantities per cylinder ring.

Why they are 0: 
Our database tracks ring height, diameter, and thickness, but not circumference subdivision into arc-plates.

*** STRATEGIC QUESTIONS TO ASK THE COMPANY ***
1) "What maximum plate roll length does our fabrication shop use for Standpipe cones and cylinders 
   (e.g., 30 ft, 35 ft, or 40 ft max plate length)?"
2) "Would you like our software to automatically calculate Ring Plate Quantities and Degrees based on that 
   max shop length (e.g., if circumference is 90 ft and max length is 35 ft, auto-divide into 3 plates of 
   120 degrees each), or do you prefer a manual override grid in the UI?"

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CATEGORY 3: Spheroid, Toroidal & Drywell Components (Inactive for Standpipes)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
*These belong to complex multi-curvature, fluted-column, or toroidal elevated water tanks.*

Columns:
- T1 to T6 (All properties): Torus Ring Segments (Standpipes do not have toroidal belly rings).
- KNUCKLE, BK, TK: Knuckle Transitions (Standpipes do not have curved knuckle transitions).
- RF (All properties): Roof Finger Plates (Used in specific radial roof structures on spheroid tanks).
- RC (All properties): Internal Reducer Cones & Rings (Standpipes do not utilize internal reducer cones).
- DW, DWSTF: Drywell Cylinders & Stiffeners (Central drywell tubes are specific to toroidal/elevated tanks).

Why they are 0: 
We are currently designing a Single Column / Standpipe! Standpipes are vertical cylinders; they do not 
have toroidal belly rings, internal drywell access tubes, or curved knuckle transitions.

*** STRATEGIC QUESTIONS TO ASK THE COMPANY ***
1) "When exporting a Standpipe or Single-Leg tank, please confirm that leaving these Spheroid-specific columns 
   as 0 is the exact expected behavior for your shop macros and Excel templates. (We kept all 107 columns 
   intact specifically to maintain universal template structure)."
2) "As we plan our roadmap for the next development phase, which tank configuration should we integrate 
   next into our database and solver — Spheroid Tanks, Toroidal/Fluted Column Tanks, or Elevated Pedestal Tanks?"
